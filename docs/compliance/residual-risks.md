# Residual risks

Risks Paddock leaves to the operator after its own mitigations (plan M6b decision 5). Record each entry in your
ISMS risk register with an owner and a decision (accept, mitigate further, transfer); the column *Record in the
ISMS* says what the decision covers. Controls and evidence: [iso27001-mapping.md](iso27001-mapping.md). Sources
are the architecture (`docs/architecture.md`), the ADRs (`docs/adr/`), the plans (`docs/plans/`) and the runbooks
(`docs/operations/`); a later release adds its new risks here.

## Devices and local root

| ID | Risk | Mitigation in Paddock | Record in the ISMS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RR-01 | **Root bypass.** Users keep local administrator rights. A root user can stop the agent, fleetd or `paddock-revoke`, edit protected files or the logs on the device; device-side evidence is not tamper-proof at the source. | Near-real-time event shipping; a missing heartbeat is itself visible (staleness, attention list); every 5 minutes the agent re-plans its bundle and corrects and reports drift of every managed resource; dedicated tamper checks for sudoers, privileged groups, keyslots, the local administrator, the PAM deny-list lines and `orbit.service` (architecture §11.5); fleetd reports a stopped agent (`device.tamper_agent_not_running` after 15 minutes without check-in); a 50 MiB spool drops tamper events last; everything stored on the server is out of the user's reach; a hole in a device's event numbering or a restarted counter is itself an audit event. | Accept that a device's own evidence ends where root starts; define the reaction to tamper events and silent devices. |
| RR-02 | **Clone detection without TPM.** Without a TPM the device key is a file; a copied disk image can sign requests. Detection relies on the diverging sequence number (one lost response tolerated); the clone is quarantined, not cut off, so that devices fail safe. | `device.clone_suspected`, quarantine, fingerprint signals (machine-id, DMI UUID, TPM EK, root LUKS UUID). | Decide whether enrollment requires a TPM-resident key. |
| RR-03 | **Permanently offline devices.** A device that never connects again cannot be locked, updated or reconfigured. | Staleness warning (default 24 h) and critical (default 168 h, *presumed lost*), attention list with Retire, Lock or Keep; disk encryption; optional dead man's switch. | Set the thresholds and own the lost-device process. |
| RR-04 | **Devices installed without the Paddock autoinstall.** Encrypted devices installed otherwise are `unmanaged` (no escrow, no Lock); devices without TPM are `tpm_missing`. | Disk encryption state per device and on the attention list. | Decide whether such devices are allowed. |
| RR-05 | **Secrets during installation.** The enrollment configuration carries its token in plain text; autoinstall user-data carries the install disk passphrase and the token; the install passphrase stays on disk until the first escrow has completed. | Tokens with expiry and maximum uses, revocable; the agent removes the install passphrase after escrow. | Treat enrollment configurations and autoinstall images like passwords; short token lifetimes. |
| RR-56 | **The shrink is not crash-safe.** `e2fsck -f`, `resize2fs` and `lvreduce` run at the conversion boot of an in-place encryption (ADR 0039) before the system starts. They take minutes, and longer the larger and slower the disk (a full check and an inode move on a spinning disk can take well over ten minutes). A power cut or a forced power-off in that window can damage the root file system. | Mains power required when a battery exists; the splash says "Encrypting the disk - do not switch off" and the console shows each step; the unit has no timeout; `e2fsck -f -p` before; the backup acknowledgement in the dialogue; idempotent stages. | The backup policy for devices that are converted. |
| RR-57 | **Interim exposure of the conversion key and of plaintext data.** From the preparation until the agent regenerates the initrd, the conversion key lies as `/etc/paddock/encrypt-key` inside every `/boot/initrd.img-*` on the unencrypted `/boot` partition (file mode 0600 protects it only on the running system). Because a keyslot cannot be added while the header is marked for reencryption, the boot PIN or the TPM unlock is enrolled only at the first start after the background encryption completed; until then, and for as long as a user keeps skipping the PIN, the conversion key in the initrd on `/boot` is the only unlock path. The initrd copy is removed at the first full pass after the TPM keyslot exists (`dracut -f --regenerate-all` without the module, verified with `lsinitrd`). The on-disk copy `/var/lib/paddock/install-passphrase` and its LUKS keyslot are removed by the `luks` reconciler once the reencryption has completed and the recovery key and header of generation 2 are stored; the escrowed generation 1 then no longer opens the disk. What an attacker with physical access gains in the window: with the initrd copy present, the disk unlocks without PIN or TPM (read the key from `/boot`, `cryptsetup open`), so the device is as unprotected as before the conversion, and the data that the reencryption has not reached yet is readable as plaintext from the partition without any key. A user who skips the boot PIN on a converted device leaves the disk encrypted but not protected at rest for as long as the PIN is skipped. A Lock or Destroy during `encrypting` erases the key-bearing keyslot and leaves the key-less `reencrypt` keyslot behind; the confirmation (`device.revocation_confirmed`) counts key-bearing keyslots only and reports the leftover separately. The volume is unrecoverable — no header of the conversion is escrowed — and the data the reencryption had not reached stays readable. | The background phase at disk speed; the device reports `key_on_boot` and `enrollment_deferred`, the device page says so, and `encrypting` and `key_on_boot` keep the device on the attention list (`disk_not_compliant`); the desktop notification asks for the restart before the encryption and again once it completed; the device asks for the PIN at every start while it is skipped; the regeneration minutes after the enrollment; the portal cancel or `paddockd encrypt-abort` for a device that will not be restarted soon. A Lock or Destroy: the dialogue says that it cannot be undone with a stored header; the agent stops the background encryption before it hands the revocation over and never restarts it afterwards, also when `paddock-revoke` refuses the revocation (the conversion stays ended on the device until a re-enrollment or reset); the result was verified on a throwaway VM and on real hardware (PDK-400). | Devices in `encrypting` or `prepared` for longer than a day; devices whose `key_on_boot` stays true for longer than a day; the rule that a converted laptop is not taken off premises before `compliant`. |
| RR-58 | **A device on initramfs-tools switches to dracut irreversibly.** initramfs-tools and `brltty` are removed; kernel updates use dracut; no Plymouth splash; the dracut initrd is built with `hostonly` and verified with `lsinitrd` before the restart, but a hardware driver dracut leaves out would stop the boot before anything is encrypted. | The dialogue says so; the allow list of the preparation refuses any other package removal; the same stack as autoinstalled 24.04 devices; gate S-IE2 proves the switch on a Desktop install; the runbook's recovery (boot the previous kernel entry; every installed kernel's initrd carries the module). | The support statement for converted devices and the allow list. |
| RR-59 | **Battery-only devices never complete.** The background phase pauses on battery. A laptop whose only charger is USB-C (power supply type `USB`, no ACPI `Mains` supply) is always seen as on battery, so its init step always skips with `on_battery`: this fails safe, but such a device can never be converted. | The pause is shown; `encrypting` keeps the device on the attention list (`disk_not_compliant`). | Who follows up. |
| RR-60 | **Only the root partition is encrypted.** Swap partitions, data partitions and `/boot` stay plaintext; hibernation images on an unencrypted swap partition contain memory. | The dialogue says so; the preflight does not refuse such layouts. | Whether devices with extra partitions are converted or reinstalled. |
| RR-61 | **Unsupported layouts stay unencrypted.** No separate `/boot` (Ubuntu Desktop's default), xfs, btrfs, RAID, no TPM: reinstall remains the path. | The reasons are shown per device. | The reinstall deadline (as RR-52). |
| RR-71 | **Freed blocks of `/boot` can hold the conversion key.** Blocks freed by a rename over an older initrd or by unlinking a name are not zeroed; zeroing does not reliably erase data on copy-on-write file systems (btrfs is not even written), on SSDs (wear levelling, TRIM) or with ext4 `data=journal`. | The key is worthless once its keyslot (the install keyslot) is wiped: right after the regenerated initrd is verified, the agent runs the `luks` reconciler's pass, which wipes the keyslot and then shreds the key file, so the window is normally seconds; after a crash in between, or while a precondition of the reconciler does not hold (its error in the health), it lasts until the next successful pass. It is far smaller than the window that exists by design (RR-57). The abort path is not affected: no keyslot ever existed for that key. | Devices whose health reports a `luks` reconciler error after the encryption completed. |
| RR-72 | **The initrd emergency shell has no password.** On devices whose initrd is built by dracut (found on Ubuntu 24.04 in the in-place encryption's hand tests), the initrd emergency shell — `rd.systemd.unit=emergency.target` on the kernel command line, or any boot whose root does not appear — gives a root shell after "Press Enter for maintenance" without a password. | Physical access only; the shell opens no encrypted volume, so encrypted data stays protected by the PIN, the TPM or an escrowed key. During an in-place encryption the not yet encrypted rest of the root partition is readable and `/boot` (the initrd, the conversion key until the regeneration) is open to manipulation anyway (RR-57). | Whether a GRUB password and a firmware password block kernel command-line edits; physical access control for devices in `prepared` or `encrypting`. |
| RR-73 | **The expected TPM enrollment after an in-place encryption is not tampering.** While the agent expects the first-boot enrollment — a conversion completed without a TPM keyslot, a user keeps skipping the boot PIN, or the TPM enrollment failed three times — one added TPM keyslot of the configured unlock mode is recorded without `tamper.keyslot_changed`, whoever added it and with any PCR policy (only the kind is compared). | Every other keyslot change stays tampering; the expectation ends with the first recorded TPM keyslot and is withdrawn at a revocation hand-over or when the conversion is cleared; the new header is escrowed as after any keyslot change. | Whether a local administrator's own TPM enrollment in that window is acceptable (RR-01). |

## Login and identity

| ID | Risk | Mitigation in Paddock | Record in the ISMS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RR-06 | **Offline login window and no MFA offline.** Offline logins (Himmelblau) use the cached Hello PIN without a second factor. A device that is offline when a user is locked keeps accepting the user's PIN until its next contact. | A lock acts in Authentik at once and on the device at its next check-in (≤ 6 minutes with jitter), where a deny list refuses logins and screen unlock offline as well; offline emergency access (password only) is off by default and warned about. | Document the absence of offline MFA and the exposure window; decide on offline emergency access. |
| RR-07 | **Himmelblau's synthetic NSS entries.** Himmelblau's NSS module answers any name, so NSS cannot tell whether a user exists. | Local-account decisions use `/etc/passwd`, `/etc/group`, `/etc/shadow` only; sudo for an unresolved user or a UID below 60000 is refused (`device.sudo_user_unresolved`); a directory name is accepted only if it resolves to the session's UID; `idmap_range` 200000–999999999. | Never change `idmap_range`; watch `device.sudo_user_unresolved`. |
| RR-08 | **Authentik is part of the trust chain.** Step-up proofs for escrow reveals and for Lock and Destroy are Authentik-signed ID tokens; whoever controls Authentik's signing key or an Authentik superuser can mint them. An Authentik superuser also sees all organizations. | The subject must be an `org_admin` in Paddock's database; MFA and `auth_time` ≤ 300 s checked; at most 5 decryptions per token; revocation limits; Destroy needs two identities; every grant and revocation of a Paddock role group membership and of Authentik superuser status is an audit event; Authentik's own event log is not ingested *(planned: AUD-03, not scheduled)*. | Treat Authentik as trusted core: pin and patch it, restrict superusers to named platform operators, hand `akadmin` to the custodians. |
| RR-09 | **Group sync lag.** Membership changes in an upstream directory reach Paddock within one sync round (5 minutes); sudo rights follow with the next bundle. Locks are not affected. | Sync every 5 minutes; locks are immediate. | Accept the lag or manage critical memberships in Paddock. |
| RR-10 | **Empty login assignment.** A device without login assignment admits every user of the organization. | The portal says so at the assignment. | Decide the default for shared and personal devices. |
| RR-91 | **One identity now spans both scopes.** A compromised two-role identity reaches the platform and its organization; before ADR 0045 that needed two accounts. | MFA at every sign-in; a fresh WebAuthn step-up per privileged action in every session; one role per session, a switch is a new sign-in through Authentik and one `admin.login` event with `previous_role`; the worker revokes each role on its own. | Which identities hold both roles; review `admin.login` events with `previous_role`. |
| RR-92 | **The alternate role is a snapshot.** The menu offers the switch from the groups seen at sign-in or at the last step-up; a role removed in Authentik meanwhile is still offered until the switch re-resolves the groups and refuses it (`role_not_granted`); no access results. | The switch always re-resolves the `groups` claim; the step-up refreshes the snapshot. | None. |
| RR-93 | **A switch is recorded in the scope it enters.** The `admin.login` of the new session (with `previous_role`) is recorded in the new role's scope: an organization auditor sees the identity's departure to the platform only through the next organization sign-in. | `previous_role` on the event; platform administrators see both scopes' events. | Whether the ISMS needs a departure event per scope (then a follow-up decision). |

## Revocation and the dead man's switch

| ID | Risk | Mitigation in Paddock | Record in the ISMS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RR-13 | **Revocation is off by default.** `PADDOCK_REVOCATION_ENABLED=false` until an installation has passed the second-person review and the hardware acceptance protocol. | `./paddock check` refuses the flag without `PADDOCK_REVOCATION_ACCEPTED=yes`. | Record the acceptance (protocol, laptop models, reviewers) before enabling. |
| RR-14 | **TPM lockout tested on emulated hardware.** Lockout and PCR behaviour were tested with swtpm (3 failures, 1 000 s per failure, survives reboots); PCR 7 binding breaks after Secure Boot DB/DBX or firmware updates; the recovery key is accepted at the PIN prompt only after the TPM attempts are used up. | Hardware acceptance protocol (`docs/operations/revocation-acceptance.md`); recovery path documented (`docs/operations/disk-recovery.md`). | Run the protocol on every laptop model and record the measured behaviour. |
| RR-15 | **Dead man's switch versus platform outages.** With the switch on, every device locks itself after the period of uptime without reaching Paddock — also when Paddock itself is down that long. The self-lock token is on the device before it is needed, so a root user could lock their own device early. | Period at least 7 days (portal warns below 30), warnings 3 and 1 days before, only uptime counts, turning it off cancels the tokens, `device.presumed_self_locked`; early self-lock only harms the user's own device and is audited. | Choose a period above the longest tolerable outage and the longest absence of users. |
| RR-16 | **Revocation keys pinned on first use.** Devices enrolled before M4c pinned the revocation keys from their first bundle that carried them (trust on first use). | `device.revocation_trust_pinned_tofu` per device; re-enrollment replaces the keys; newer devices pin from the enrollment configuration only. | List the affected devices; re-enroll or accept. |
| RR-17 | **A Lock does not cover every volume.** A Lock erases the root volume and the volumes with an escrowed header only. Skipped and readable after a Lock: a volume whose newest header generation is still being escrowed; volumes beyond the **32-volume cap** (a device escrows at most 32 volume headers, a token lists at most 32, the first by UUID; a 33rd is refused for 24 hours unless the crypttab volumes change); **shared-UUID volumes** (a volume sharing its LUKS UUID with another volume or with root, e.g. a cloned header, is neither escrowed nor tracked and is reported as `shared_uuid`); all volumes other than root when `paddock-revoke` cannot read the root volume's UUID. A Destroy erases all of them. | Every skipped volume is reported in the device's revocation timeline (`skipped_not_escrowed`) and the disk encryption card; volumes are listed per device. | Know that a Lock is only as complete as the escrow; use Destroy for devices whose data must become unreadable. Details: `docs/operations/revocation.md`, *Limits and accepted residual risk*. |
| RR-18 | **Forged escrow on a compromised device (accepted).** The server cannot verify the content of a sealed header. A root user can have a volume counted as escrowed with a forged header, or report a false root UUID; a Lock then makes that volume unrecoverable. The device cannot add volumes outside `paddock-revoke`'s own crypttab selection and cannot weaken a Destroy. | Restorability is the operator's concern only for devices whose root is trusted. | Accept, as recorded in the revocation runbook. |
| RR-19 | **`paddock-revoke` downgrade.** After a downgrade to a build without volume support, Lock and self-lock tokens are refused (nothing erased); older builds erase every volume on a Lock. | `device.revocation_refused` (`malformed`); capability reported per device. | Keep `paddock-revoke` current before relying on restorable Locks. |
| RR-20 | **Data loss on revocation.** A Lock or a `terminate` user lock ends sessions; unsaved work is lost. The recovery key stays valid after a reveal. | Confirmation dialogs with typed hostname; Destroy needs a second administrator; limits per administrator (3 per hour, 10 per 24 h), per organization (20 per 24 h) and per device (1 per 24 h). | Put the consequences into the user policy; mass revocations need a platform administrator to raise limits. |

## Configuration as code and API tokens

| ID | Risk | Mitigation in Paddock | Record in the ISMS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RR-24 | **API token outlives its creator's rights for a while.** A token stops working once its creator's account is disabled or lost the role the token needs, but Paddock learns that only at the creator's next step-up or from the worker within the identity sync interval; until then, and for a creator locked only in a way the directory does not show, the token keeps working, without MFA, until it expires or is revoked. | Mandatory expiry (1 hour to 365 days, portal default 90 days); a step-up for every creation, so a token cannot create tokens; any organization administrator can revoke any token, effective with the next request; the creator is shown. | Review and revoke tokens when an administrator leaves; give CI the narrowest role. |
| RR-25 | **API token secrets in CI.** A token is as safe as the CI system that stores it; wrong secrets are not rate-limited (256-bit secrets). | Metric `paddock_api_token_auth_total{result="unknown"}`. | Store tokens in the CI secret store; alert on unknown-token attempts. |
| RR-26 | **Section-authoritative deletes.** In a declarative apply (`paddockctl apply`, `PUT /api/v1/config`) a section that is present is authoritative: items it does not list are deleted, a renamed key is a delete plus a create. | Dry run first; deletions need `--yes`; the apply carries the reviewed plan's SHA-256 (`expected_plan`); the CI example runs the dry run on pull requests. | Make plan review part of change control. |

## Control plane and audit domain

| ID | Risk | Mitigation in Paddock | Record in the ISMS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RR-27 | **Young WORM implementation.** The bundled audit store RustFS is young and had an Object Lock enforcement CVE before 1.0 (ADR 0017). | The WORM acceptance gate runs on every acceptance run and every RustFS upgrade; Ceph RGW or an external S3 with Object Lock can replace it; `./paddock check --online` (audit host) checks COMPLIANCE retention ≥ 400 days. | Choose the store (strict audit requirements: Ceph RGW or external S3); record gate results; replicate the bucket. |
| RR-28 | **OpenBao unseal shares and keys.** OpenBao starts sealed after every restart; while sealed, bundle signing, commands, reveals and the daily audit seal stop (devices keep working). The backup encryption key has only one copy on the control plane besides the offline copy; AppRole secret IDs do not expire. | Shamir 3 of 5 with named custodians and PGP-encrypted shares; root token revoked after setup; alerts `PaddockOpenBaoSealed` and unreachable; optional KMS auto-unseal (not bundled). | Name the custodians and the share mapping; keep the backup key offline with them; rotate secret IDs at least yearly; decide on auto-unseal. |
| RR-29 | **Per-host internal CA.** `./paddock secrets` creates an internal CA per host (valid 10 years; leaf certificates 825 days). Its key stays on the host in `.secrets/internal-ca/`; interconnect certificates are not covered by the TLS expiry probe. | TLS on every cross-host link, `prod-check` fails on plaintext links, endpoints only on the interconnect address. | Custody and backup of the CA keys; renew the certificates before 825 days. |
| RR-30 | **Single-host availability.** The production topology is one control-plane host and one audit host; RabbitMQ, Valkey and OpenBao run as single nodes (HA is an operator topology, not automated). | Devices fail safe (C2); encrypted backups outside the host; restore drill (RTO 4 h for PostgreSQL, OpenBao and escrow; RPO 5 min PostgreSQL, 6 h OpenBao). | Accept the availability or build HA; record RTO and RPO; run the restore drill quarterly. |
| RR-31 | **Backups and restore scope.** Fleet's database is dumped daily (RPO 24 h); bundles and Valkey are rebuilt, not restored; the audit store is not part of the restore (it is replicated instead); OpenBao secret IDs issued after the restored snapshot must be issued again. | `docs/operations/restore.md`; `make restore-drill`; backup age alert. | Provide the off-host backup bucket; record each drill. |
| RR-32 | **Presigned URLs and edge caching.** Bundle URLs are valid 120 s, header upload URLs 10 minutes; a CDN may cache them by full URL. | Bundles are signed and immutable; object keys are bound to organization, device and version. | Configure any CDN with the full URL as cache key. |
| RR-33 | **One binary with many roles; broker outages.** A bug in shared code affects every role; a RabbitMQ or Valkey outage stops ingest and check-ins. | Role-specific OpenBao and database credentials and one container per role; devices keep working and spool 50 MiB of events; idempotent consumers. | Monitor the roles; accept the shared code base. |
| RR-34 | **Capacity not yet measured on your hardware.** | Load scenarios and method in `docs/operations/capacity.md`. | Measure before large rollouts; record the figures. |
| RR-35 | **Portal session state in Valkey.** Portal sessions need their record in Valkey: while Valkey is unreachable the portal answers 503 (devices and API tokens keep working; signing out still clears the browser's cookie). A role change, a removal or a deactivation in the identity provider ends the sessions of an organization or platform administrator within the worker's identity sync interval (5 minutes by default); logout, a step-up whose groups no longer grant the role and *End all sessions* end them at once. Revocations run in Valkey inside the database transaction of their audit event, before it commits: if that commit fails, the sessions are ended but the event records a failure. *End all sessions* also ends the identity provider's browser sessions of every portal user; the identity provider's sessions of a single account stay when only that account's Paddock sessions are revoked (role change, step-up mismatch), because the account simply signs in again with its current role. Valkey writes its append-only file every second (`appendfsync everysec`), so a Valkey crash within that second can lose the latest generation increment or revocation and make the ended sessions valid again. The worker confirms a change with a direct read of the user by username and ID: when no user of that username with the account's ID exists any more (deleted, renamed, or the username now held by another user) it counts the administrator as removed, so a deleted user loses access; an administrator renamed in the identity provider is disabled instead of moved to a new role until the next login corrects the account; only a failed read changes nothing. | Valkey with AOF; cookies are bound to their record, generation, account, role and organization; a login opens its session while it holds the account row, so a concurrent revocation cannot miss it; the worker matches accounts by the directory's user ID, confirms every change with a direct read of the user and changes nothing when the role groups cannot be read; it repeats revocations every interval. | Monitor Valkey; after a Valkey crash right after ending sessions, end all sessions again; record the sync interval. |
| RR-36 | **Agents older than the path tiers and denied paths.** From the release with the managed-file path tiers (ADR 0034) the server no longer ships managed files on denied paths. An agent of that release or newer keeps such a file unchanged and stops tracking it; an agent older than that release deletes every unchanged file that leaves its bundle, so it deletes those files at its next check-in once the server is upgraded. | Binding upgrade order for that release: agents first, then the server (`docs/operations/upgrade.md`, `install.md`); the security review lists the definitions on denied paths, so administrators remove them deliberately. | Roll the agent out to every device before the server upgrade; accept the loss of unchanged files on denied paths for devices that return later with an older agent, or redeploy them. |
| RR-37 | **Operator roots are a trust decision.** Below an operator root an operator manages files without a step-up (ADR 0034 amendment 2026-10-16). A program that runs as root and sources or loads suffixless files below that root (configuration it executes, includes it evaluates) makes the root root-equivalent, which the path catalog cannot see. | Roots are empty by default and set only by an organization administrator with a step-up (`settings.managed_files_changed`); never-operator directories are refused; executables, scripts, shared objects and `.d` directories stay sensitive below a root; the portal page warns. | Grant an operator root only for application configuration that no root-run program executes; review the roots with the audit log. |
| RR-38 | **The step-up factor depends on the identity provider's login event.** Paddock refuses a step-up whose token does not name a WebAuthn authenticator in `paddock_mfa_classes`; that claim is computed by a Paddock-managed mapping from the login event Authentik keeps for the session. An Authentik administrator who changes the mapping, the step-up stage or the login event's content can make TOTP pass again. | The blueprints `paddock-stepup.yaml` and `paddock-portal.yaml` are applied on every Authentik start and restore the mapping and the stages; a token without the claim is refused; the portal sign-in enrols WebAuthn for every administrator who has none; Authentik's admin actions are logged by Authentik. | Restrict Authentik administration; review Authentik's event log for changes to the step-up flow and the Paddock mappings. |
| RR-39 | **Upstream membership completes a root grant.** Paddock judges a grant to a group before anyone holds it, through a stand-in member of the group (plan M8s decisions 7g-quinquies and 7g-sexies), but membership that comes from the upstream directory is not gated: a user who joins an imported group upstream receives its grants with the next identity sync, and the members that an import mirrors when an imported group is created arrive the same way, without a step-up. | A grant to the group itself, and every membership change made in Paddock, needs an organization administrator with a step-up when it gives anyone a new way to root; the sync and the import are audited (`user.synced_added`, `user_group.created` with its member count) and the security review lists the effective root grants. | Treat membership of upstream groups that carry sudo grants as a privileged change in the upstream directory; review the audit log of the sync and of group imports. |
| RR-40 | **Hello PIN re-enrolment after 30 days without a PIN authentication, and on agents older than 0.1.0-alpha.4.** Himmelblau 4.0.x re-seals the device login's refresh token with the Hello key only at a PIN authentication and presents the sealed token at the next one; a background refresh that renewed the token in between makes the next PIN login fail, and Himmelblau deletes the Hello key. | The agent sets Himmelblau's `cache_timeout` to the refresh token's validity (30 days, `docs/operations/device-login.md`, *Tokens of the device login*), so the background refresh runs only when the sealed token has expired anyway; every PIN authentication re-seals the token and restarts the clock. The device provider's `refresh_token_threshold` stays at zero. | RR-43 | **Automatic unseal puts three unseal shares on the control-plane host.** With automatic unseal enabled (ADR 0035), the unsealer holds three of the five shares in `.secrets/openbao/auto-unseal-shares`. | RR-62 | **Portal sign-in needs a WebAuthn authenticator, and the first enrolment trusts the password.** An administrator whose browser or device has no WebAuthn authenticator (no security key, no platform authenticator, no phone the browser can use) cannot complete the portal sign-in once the WebAuthn enrolment is due; the enrolment applies only to members of Paddock's administrator groups (platform administrators and the administrators, operators and auditors of every organization), so a device user who approves a device sign-in on the device-code page, which runs the same sign-in, is not asked to enrol a passkey (ADR 0034 amendment 2026-10-29). Whoever holds the password and two recovery codes of such an account approves a device-code login of that user with the QR code on a device the user may sign in to (three codes when the code is typed); a used code is spent. A user outside the administrator groups who lost a TOTP authenticator that is still registered can sign in to the own device this way without enrolling a passkey. | The portal sign-in asks for an existing authenticator (WebAuthn or TOTP) first and enrols WebAuthn only for an account without any authenticator, or after the TOTP code or one of the account's recovery codes for an account with TOTP only (plan M8f decision 13 as amended on 2026-10-18); Authentik's own interfaces and the device login keep accepting TOTP, so the administrator can still manage authenticators in Authentik; Authentik's own sign-in skips Paddock's WebAuthn/TOTP stage only after a WebAuthn or TOTP validation in the same sign-in, so a recovery code, e-mail, SMS or Duo code is followed by a second prompt for a factor of the user's own devices (or, without TOTP or WebAuthn, by the enrolment), where Authentik accepts a further recovery code as well (PDK-324). Every enrolment of a WebAuthn authenticator, in either sign-in or in the user settings, is recorded in Authentik's event log (*Events* → *Logs*, action `model_created`, model `webauthndevice`); it is not part of Paddock's audit trail. Administrators are created by a platform administrator. | Issue security keys or approve platform authenticators for every administrator; keep one platform administrator's key in custody; create an administrator shortly before the first sign-in, hand the password over separately and check the enrolment in Authentik's event log. | RR-64 | **TLS alone protects the confidentiality of reports and of the enrollment token, and the login component and fleetd trust the system CA store.** Health and inventory reports are signed, not encrypted; the enrollment token travels in the enrollment configuration; Himmelblau (OIDC to Authentik) and fleetd (to Fleet) verify TLS against the system CA store and pin nothing. | RR-65 | **A withheld configuration is visible after an hour, and a blocked connection can trigger the dead man's switch.** An attacker who proxies a device's connection can withhold newer bundles (for example a revoked sudo right) for up to an hour before the device appears as *Configuration behind*; blocking the connection entirely makes the device stale and, with the dead man's switch enabled, locks it after the period. | The attention kind `config_behind` (one hour after the newest bundle, with a check-in after that hour); staleness alerts; the dead man's switch setting states the consequence. | React to *Configuration behind* and staleness; choose the switch period with blocking in mind. |
